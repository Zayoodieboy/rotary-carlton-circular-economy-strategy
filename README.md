## 🧵 Part A - Problem Description: Textile Waste

Textile waste is a severe problem resulting in a significant negative environmental impact in Australia (M.A. Bukhari 2018)[cite: 6]. Out of 100 million garment products, a staggering 92 million tons of textile waste are generated annually (Igina 2022)[cite: 6]. Only 15% of these clothes are recycled, while the remainder ends up in landfills (Kelly 2022)[cite: 6]. Textile waste takes up to 200 years to decompose due to its high fiber content of up to 69% (Style 2022)[cite: 6]. The fashion industry now ranks as the fourth largest environmental polluter globally with its intensive use of energy, water, and chemicals (J. Riba 2020)[cite: 6].

### Causes
* **Fast Fashion:** Excessive textile production creates a constant desire for new collections, leading consumers to feel outdated and buy more (Saxon 2022)[cite: 6]. Retail giants like Zara and H&M use this strategy to attract young customers and generate media attention (Barnes 2006)[cite: 6].
* **International Migration:** Increases overconsumption and lowers the 3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle) rate (Jr 2023)[cite: 6]. In 2022, overseas migration in Australia increased by 171,000 (ABS 2022)[cite: 6].
* **Skyrocketing Consumption:** 80 billion garments are purchased each year, representing a 400% increase compared to previous decades (Morgan 2015)[cite: 7].

### Effects
The main effects of textile waste include water, microfiber, air, and soil pollution[cite: 7]. Toxic substances (including mercury and arsenic) are dumped into rivers, threatening human health (Noyes 2022)[cite: 7]. Cotton cultivation exacerbates water scarcity, requiring 9,700 liters for every kilogram of cotton (Leahy 2015)[cite: 7]. Furthermore, 35% of microplastic pollution originates from fashion, entering the food chain and leading to diseases (Milton 2023)[cite: 7].

## 📊 Part B - Analysis of the External Business Environment

### PESTEL Analysis
* **Political (P):** Supported by stable government funding, the National Clothing Product Stewardship Scheme, charitable tax deductions, and innovation encouragement (DCCEEW 2022; ATO 2018; Australian Fashion Council 2023)[cite: 10].
* **Economic (E):** Post-pandemic economic recovery, low unemployment rates (3.5%), and rising disposable incomes stimulate consumer spending and demand for affordable second-hand clothing (ABS 2023; RBA 2023)[cite: 10, 25].
* **Social (S):** Carlton's young and diverse population (median age 27, with 25.7% aged 20–29) and growing environmental awareness provide an ideal target customer base (ABS 2021)[cite: 10, 26].
* **Technological (T):** Digital technologies, mobile apps, social media marketing, and automated warehousing systems enhance operational efficiency and reach (Forbes 2022; McKinsey 2023)[cite: 10, 27].
* **Legal (L):** Strict compliance required regarding ACCC Competition Law, Health and Safety Regulations 2017, and app/website launching requirements (ACCC 2022; Government of Victoria 2017)[cite: 10, 27].
* **Environmentally (E):** Focusing on carbon footprint reduction, proper recycling procedures, and sustainability builds a strong green brand image (DCCEEW 2022; Sarkar 2019)[cite: 10, 11, 28].

### Porter's Five Forces Model
* **Threat of New Entrants (Low):** High startup costs, specialist knowledge requirements, economies of scale, and stringent regulatory barriers (Amelia et al. 2019)[cite: 11, 28].
* **Bargaining Power of Buyers (Moderate):** Price-sensitive consumers may switch to alternative recyclers or new clothing if prices are too high (Goldsmith & Newell 1997)[cite: 11, 29].
* **Bargaining Power of Suppliers (Low):** High availability of commercial recyclers and sustainable local markets provides strong supply chain stability (Sandberg et al. 2018)[cite: 11, 29].
* **Threat of Substitutes (High):** Traditional fast-fashion retail options and new clothing alternatives perform well and pose constant substitution pressure (Brace-Govan & Binay 2010)[cite: 11, 30].
* **Competitive Rivalry Among Competitors (High):** Tight competition exists among non-profit service organizations capturing similar market share with fluctuating customer loyalty (Amelia et al. 2019)[cite: 11, 30].

## 🥊 Part C - Competitor Analysis

The competitive landscape includes key non-profit service organizations operating nationally and locally:
1. **Lions Club:** Focuses on sight preservation, disaster relief, and community development (Key projects: *SightFirst*, *Lions Quest*)[cite: 11, 12].
2. **Kiwanis Club:** Focuses on serving children worldwide through health, education, and community development (Key projects: *Eliminate Project*, *Key Club*)[cite: 12].
3. **Freemasons:** Fraternal organization focused on personal development, charitable giving, and community service (Key projects: *Masonic Charitable Foundation*)[cite: 12].
4. **Rotary Club of Carlton (RCC):** Addresses diverse social, environmental, and humanitarian issues with a volunteer-driven professional network (Key projects: *Polio eradication*, *Clean water & sanitation*)[cite: 12, 13].

### SWOT Analysis Summary
* **Strengths:** Global brand recognition, strong professional network, and flexibility in addressing diverse social/environmental issues[cite: 13].
* **Weaknesses:** Heavy reliance on member donations and aging volunteer base; difficulty engaging younger members[cite: 13].
* **Opportunities:** Ecosystem collaboration with community organizations and digital platform expansion targeting younger audiences[cite: 13].
* **Threats:** Cost-of-living increases impacting donations and younger demographics having limited disposable time/income[cite: 13].

## 💡 Part D - Solutions & 3-Phase Recommendations

To adopt ecosystem growth strategies (Thompson et al. 2018), RCC will partner with external organizations to fundraise and promote a circular economy[cite: 13, 15]:

* **Recommendation 1 — Collect Clothes with UPPAREL:** Partner with commercial textile recycler UPPAREL to manage home collection logistics and processing for unused clothing starting at $25 per box[cite: 15].
* **Recommendation 2 — Repurpose Clothes with The Social Studio:** Collaborate with local designers and young fashion professionals to transform collected textiles into new garments, accessories (bags, scarves), or home textiles[cite: 15, 16].
* **Recommendation 3 — Host a Fundraising Workshop Event with TFIA:** Partner with the Council of Textile and Fashion Industries of Australia (TFIA) to host an educational sustainable fashion workshop in Parkville, featuring guest speakers, up-cycling masterclasses, and a silent auction/POS sales terminal for repurposed collections[cite: 16, 18, 68].
